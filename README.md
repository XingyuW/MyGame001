# MyGame001

An original single-player real-time strategy game for Apple Silicon macOS, in the tradition of the classic
isometric base-building RTS: deploy your base, harvest ore, produce an army, and defeat the computer
opponents before they defeat you. It offers skirmish matches with the sides, countries and lobby
options of Red Alert 2 on twenty-one maps, from small duels to large battlefields with cities,
coasts and different environments, against one to nine computer players at three difficulties.

The game is written in Rust with [Bevy](https://bevyengine.org) 0.19. All rules live in the
`sim` crate (integer arithmetic, deterministic, tested headlessly); the `game` crate draws the
simulation and turns input into commands. `PLAN.md` is the project plan, `docs/DECISIONS.md` the
decision log, and `docs/STATUS.md` the milestone reports.

## Sides and countries

A match is fought between the two sides of Red Alert 2, each player with one of nine countries
(`assets/data/rules.ron`, `skirmish.ron`; the values of every item are those of the original
rules, generated by `scripts/generate_ra2_data.py` from the reference tables under
`crates/sim/tests/reference/`):

| Side | Countries and what only they build |
|------|------------------------------------|
| Allied | **America** (its own Airforce Command in place of the common one), **Korea** (Black Eagle in place of the Harrier), **France** (Grand Cannon), **Germany** (Tank Destroyer), **Great Britain** (Sniper) |
| Soviet | **Libya** (Demolition Truck), **Iraq** (Desolator), **Cuba** (Terrorist), **Russia** (Tesla Tank) |

Each side has its own Construction Yard, Barracks, Refinery, War Factory, Naval Shipyard, Service
Depot, Battle Lab and walls, and its own units; until the Soviet buildings and the new units have
art of their own they show that of their Allied twins. What you may build is decided as in Red
Alert 2: you own a factory of the item's kind (Construction Yard, Barracks, War Factory, Naval
Shipyard, Airforce Command) of a side that builds it, you own its prerequisites, and your country
is not excluded. Because the rule looks at your buildings, **capturing** an enemy Barracks or War
Factory with an Engineer lets you build what that factory builds, whatever your side; a country's
special stays that country's.

The sidebar has four tabs, **Buildings | Armory | Infantry | Vehicles** (aircraft and ships are
under Vehicles). A tab lists only what your buildings could build: nothing before your MCV has
deployed, the infantry of your side once you own a Barracks, a Conscript once you own a captured
Soviet Barracks. A card whose prerequisites you can still build is shown **LOCKED**; hover it for
what it needs. Cards show cost, missing prerequisites, queue progress and ready or blocked
states. Queues charge as work proceeds; cancellation refunds exactly the credits already charged.
A completed building waits for valid placement.

## Production, power and base building

These follow Red Alert 2 since milestone RA2 of the parity programme (times are at game speed
1×, 20 ticks a second).

* **Build time.** Every item takes `cost × 21 / 25` ticks: 42 seconds per 1000 credits. A Power
  Plant (800) takes 34 seconds, a Grizzly (700) 29, a War Factory (2000) 84; a wall three times
  its share (13 seconds for 100 credits). The credits are charged as the work proceeds, and
  work waits while you cannot pay.
* **Several factories.** Every further factory of a kind (Construction Yards, Barracks, War
  Factories, Naval Shipyards, Airforce Commands; either side's count) builds that kind's items
  1.25 times as fast, up to ten: two War Factories build a Grizzly in 24 seconds.
* **Lanes and queues.** The Construction Yard builds two things at once: one **building**
  (Buildings tab) and one **defence** (Armory tab: walls, turrets, the Gap Generator, the Spy
  Satellite, the Psychic Sensor and the superweapons). Each of these two lanes holds one item:
  while it is being built, or is READY and waits to be placed, the other cards of its tab say
  `busy`. Barracks, War Factories, Shipyards and Airforce Commands have a queue each, which
  holds the unit in production and 29 behind it. A thin bar on each tab shows the progress of
  its lane, whichever tab is open.
* **Build limits.** One Ore Purifier, Cloning Vats, Chronosphere, Weather Control Device, Iron
  Curtain Device and Nuclear Missile Silo per player; the card then says `LIMIT`.
* **Low power.** A shortage slows all production: to 80 percent for a shortage of up to a fifth
  of what your buildings consume, down to 50 percent for half or more. Only buildings that are
  powered **and** consume power stop working: Prism Tower, Tesla Coil, Patriot, Flak Cannon,
  Grand Cannon, Gap Generator, Spy Satellite, Psychic Sensor and Ore Purifier. A Pillbox, a
  Sentry Gun and the Cloning Vats keep working. Superweapons stop charging, and cannot be
  fired, until the power is back.
* **Radar.** The minimap is your radar: it needs an Airforce Command, a Radar Tower or a Spy
  Satellite Uplink, and enough power. Without one, under low power, and while an enemy spy has
  switched your power off, it shows **RADAR OFFLINE** with the reason, and clicks on it do
  nothing.
* **Placement.** A building must stand within 2 free cells of one of your base buildings; a
  Pillbox, a Sentry Gun and a Patriot within 4; a wall within 8. Defences, walls and shipyards
  are not base buildings: nothing can be placed from them alone. A Naval Shipyard may stand up
  to 31 free cells from your base (Red Alert 2 allows 12; on most shipped maps the water lies
  farther from the start than that, and the maps were not reshaped).
* **Selling and repairing.** A sale refunds half the cost. A repair (`K`, or the Repair
  button) restores 8 hit points about once a second (every 19 ticks) and charges each step, so
  that a whole repair costs about 15 percent of the price; it waits while you cannot pay. Walls
  can be neither sold nor repaired.
* **Service Depot.** Select damaged vehicles, right click your Service Depot (or press `I` and click it): they drive
  up to it and are repaired there step by step for credits, at the rate and price of a building
  repair (a Grizzly: 2 credits per 8 hit points), then make room. Any new order ends the
  repair. Ships are repaired the same way at a Naval Shipyard. Nothing is repaired for free by
  standing near a depot any more.

## Armour, warheads and weapons

Since milestone RA3 of the parity programme units and buildings fight with the armour,
warheads and weapons of Red Alert 2 (a range is in cells; a reload at game speed 1×).

* **Armour.** Every unit and building has one of eleven armour types: none (GI, Engineer,
  Rocketeer), flak (Conscript, Tanya), plate (Tesla Trooper, Desolator), light (IFV, V3,
  aircraft), medium (the miners), heavy (tanks, most ships), wood (Power Plant, Refinery, War
  Factory), steel (Barracks, the defences), concrete (Construction Yard, walls, superweapons)
  and two special ones (the Terror Drone). Point at a unit or building to read it under its
  name.
* **Warheads.** A weapon deals its damage through a warhead, which takes a percentage of it
  for the target's armour. A GI's bullets (15) deal all of it to unarmoured infantry, a
  quarter to a tank and to concrete; a Grizzly's shell (65) deals all of it to a tank, 65
  percent to a Power Plant and a quarter to infantry; a Tesla Trooper's bolt (50) all of it
  to a tank and half to buildings. What a weapon cannot damage at all it does not fire at:
  bullets do nothing to walls, an Attack Dog nothing to a vehicle.
* **Splash.** Most shells, missiles and bombs damage everything within a radius of where they
  land, less with the distance, **your own units too**: a tank's shell a third of a cell, an
  IFV's missile half a cell, a Destroyer's shell and a V3 rocket 1 cell, a Kirov's bomb and a
  Grand Cannon's shell 2 cells, a Demolition Truck 8, a nuclear missile 10. A building takes
  a hit for every cell of it inside the radius, so wide blasts are worst for large buildings:
  a nuclear strike destroys a Power Plant and leaves a Construction Yard standing with about
  three fifths of its strength.
* **Two weapons.** Some units carry a second weapon and use whichever fits the target: the
  Apocalypse its guns against the ground and its missiles against aircraft, the Flak Track,
  the Flak Trooper and the Sea Scorpion a flak gun for each, the Destroyer its gun against
  ships and its Osprey's depth charges against submarines. An IFV's missiles reach aircraft 4 cells beyond
  their range of 6.
* **What can attack what.** Aircraft are hit only by weapons for the air: flak, the Patriot,
  the Aegis, the IFV, the Apocalypse's missiles, the Rocketeer, the GI's and the tanks' guns
  not. Submerged units (Typhoon, Dolphin, Giant Squid) are hit only by the depth charges of the
  Destroyer's Osprey, the Typhoon's torpedoes, the Dolphin and the squid; they in turn attack nothing on
  land. The Sniper and Tanya shoot at vehicles and buildings only when ordered to (1 percent).
  An order to attack something a unit can never hit ends at once.
* **Bursts and minimum range.** The Black Eagle, the Harrier, the IFV and the Apocalypse fire
  volleys of two shots a quarter of a second apart and then reload. A V3 Launcher cannot fire
  nearer than 5 cells, a Dreadnought nearer than 8, a Grand Cannon nearer than 3: ordered to
  attack something closer, a unit backs away first.
* **Flak is inaccurate.** Its bursts land around the target, wider the farther it is, and
  damage what is within a cell of them.
* **Damage states.** At half its strength a unit or building is **yellow** and trails thin
  grey smoke, at a quarter **red** with thick black smoke. A vehicle or ship that is yellow or
  red moves at three quarters of its speed; infantry and aircraft do not slow down.
* **Three soldiers to a cell.** Infantry on foot stand up to three to a cell, each at a spot
  of its own (one above the middle of the cell, two below it left and right), yours and the
  enemy's alike. A vehicle needs a cell to itself: it does not enter a cell with a soldier in
  it, and a soldier does not enter a cell with a vehicle. A group of soldiers ordered to a
  place fills the cells around it three at a time. Ranges and blasts are measured to where a
  soldier stands, so a shell that lands on one of three soldiers hurts the other two a little
  less. Click on a soldier to select that one of the three; a soldier's health bar is half as
  long as a vehicle's. The Rocketeer flies and has the air above a cell to itself.
* **Crushing.** A tank, a miner, an MCV, a Flak Track, a V3 Launcher or a Soviet Amphibious
  Transport that you order to **move** or **attack move** drives over enemy infantry in its
  way, and they die under it; all the soldiers of a cell at once. It never drives over your
  own or an ally's soldiers (it goes round them, or waits), and it cannot crush a Tesla
  Trooper, a Desolator, a Chrono Legionnaire or the stolen technology infantry. An IFV, a
  Terror Drone and a Demolition Truck crush nothing. A unit ordered to **attack** does not
  crush on its way to its target, and a miner at work does not either: move your tanks through
  the infantry to crush it, and move your infantry out of the way of theirs.
* **Still the game's own until later milestones.** C4,
  the Chrono Legionnaire's erasing, the dog's and the Terror Drone's instant kills, the Giant
  Squid, mind control, the deployed GI, Desolator and Yuri, radiation after a nuclear strike,
  and the timing of the superweapons keep the simpler mechanics they had (RA6).
  `crates/sim/tests/reference/ra2_differences.tsv` lists every value that differs from Red
  Alert 2's `rules.ini`, with its reason.

## Veterancy, crews, aircraft, missiles and submarines

As in Red Alert 2 (`docs/RA2_PARITY_PLAN.md`, RA4):

* **Veterancy.** A unit gains the cost of every enemy unit and building it destroys (by its
  shot, by the splash of its shot, by crushing; a V3's rocket and a carrier's Hornets earn for
  the launcher and the carrier). At three times its own cost it is a **veteran** (one chevron
  under its health bar), at six times **elite** (three chevrons); a ping, a golden ring and a
  notice tell you. A rank gives what the unit's type lists: 10 percent more damage, half as
  much again armour, a reload of 60 percent, 20 percent more speed, and at elite rank the
  unit's elite weapon and, for most, self healing (a hit point every second). Engineers, dogs,
  spies, miners of the Allies, MCVs and transports learn nothing; buildings neither. The
  Apocalypse, the Desolator, the miners, the Kirov and the Giant Squid heal themselves from
  the start.
* **Crews and survivors.** A destroyed or sold building lets its people out: a GI or a Conscript
  for every 500 (Allies) or 250 (Soviets) credits of its selling price, at least one, and
  from a Construction Yard sometimes an Engineer. A destroyed MCV that stood still lets one
  out every other time. Defences, walls, shipyards and the Gap Generator have no crew.
* **Aircraft.** A Harrier or a Black Eagle needs one of the **four docks** of an Airforce
  Command: you cannot order a fifth until a second Airforce Command stands (the card says "No
  free dock"). It carries **one volley**, flies home by itself when it has fired or has nothing
  to do, lands on its dock (a small diamond on the building) and reloads there in 18 seconds.
  If its Airforce Command is lost it takes a free dock of another; with none it is lost.
  The NightHawk, the Kirov and the Rocketeer need no dock.
* **Missiles and carried aircraft.** A V3 Launcher fires a **rocket** and a Dreadnought two
  **missiles** that fly to where the target stood: real aircraft that Patriots, Flak and every
  other anti air weapon can shoot down, in which case they do nothing. The next is ready 27
  seconds (V3) or 5 seconds (Dreadnought) after the last is gone. An Aircraft Carrier sends its
  **three Hornets**, which drop a bomb each, return and go again; a Hornet that is shot down
  is replaced after 40 seconds, and all are lost with the carrier. A Destroyer sends its
  **Osprey** against submerged targets. You cannot select or command these aircraft.
* **Submarines.** A Typhoon, a Dolphin and a Giant Squid are **unseen** by an enemy, also in
  water the enemy sees, unless one of its Destroyers is within 8 cells (or one of its own
  submerged units within 4), or the unit fired in the last second or so. What you cannot see
  you cannot attack; the computer players cannot either.

[Construction system report](docs/CONSTRUCTION_SYSTEM.md) documents the roster, mechanics,
integration and tests as they were before the Red Alert 2 parity programme, which
`docs/RA2_PARITY_PLAN.md` describes milestone by milestone (`docs/STATUS.md` says how far it is).
The artwork and the sounds are original; no file of Red Alert 2 is part of the game.

## High-resolution battlefield presentation

The desktop client now uses a separate HD art set: **31 building identities and 53 unit
identities**, with eight facings per unit and four texture pixels per world pixel. The art
keeps an isometric battlefield, bold blue and red paint, oversized industrial equipment,
compact armour, gold hazard markings, and distinct infantry, aircraft and ship silhouettes.
Soviet reactors, Tesla equipment and heavy armour have their own red industrial designs;
Allied facilities use blue trim, pale metal, glass and cleaner shapes.

The rebuild adds detailed roof equipment, tank tracks, gantries, pipes, windows, aprons,
soft baked shadows, sixteen variations per terrain class, shoreline foam, water shimmer,
industrial exhaust, damage smoke, muzzle flashes, shell impacts, electric arcs, energy beams,
explosion clouds, shockwaves, debris and fading scorch marks. Production cards use larger HD
artwork, and closer camera zoom remains sharp. All presentation effects respect fog of war;
particle lifetimes pause with gameplay and are cleared when a match changes.

Field units are rendered as real equipment is finished: vehicles in three-colour disruptive
paint with dust, mud and chipped edges, soldiers in printed camouflage under darker
load-bearing kit, under a harder sun than the buildings and with a thin dark contour so that a
camouflaged tank still reads on ground of its own colour. Every unit that goes on foot has a
**walk cycle** (six frames in each of the eight facings, `assets/sprites/hd/walk`): the legs
stride, the knee folds as a foot swings forward, the body bobs and the shoulders turn against
the hips; commandos, irregulars and dogs run. The cycle advances with the ground covered, not
with the clock, so feet stay planted instead of sliding.

Weapons look and sound like what they are (`crates/game/src/weapon_fx.rs`,
`crates/game/src/audio.rs`). A prism weapon fires a beam of light: a white core in a blue
halo edged with the colours of a spectrum, a glare at the emitter and where it strikes, and
the ring of struck crystal. A Tesla weapon throws a lightning bolt that forks and jumps to a
new path many times a second, with the snap and 110 Hz buzz of an electric arc. Rifles crack
and send tracers, machine guns fire bursts, tank guns boom with a muzzle blast, smoke and a
dust ring, shells and rockets fly (rockets on a smoke trail, artillery on an arc) and burst
where they land with a sound of their own.

This remains a **2D isometric renderer**. Lighting and model shadows are baked into the
sprites; the animated light glows are composited effects. It does not yet have real-time 3D
shadows, physically based reflections or a general weather system.
No original game assets are bundled. Art quality and faithfulness remain subject to visual
review; the captures below show the implemented result.

Generate the original art (Blender 5.x for the pre-rendered miniatures; `uv` supplies Pillow
and NumPy). The full Blender pass of the buildings and units takes roughly an hour on Apple
Silicon:

```sh
uv run --with pillow --with numpy python scripts/generate_hd_terrain.py
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup --python scripts/render_hd_blender.py
uv run --with pillow --with numpy python scripts/finish_hd_art.py
```

Renders go to `~/Library/Caches/Codex/reviews/MyGame001/ra2-visual/render` by default; pass
`-- OUT_DIR b:<building> u:<unit> w:<unit> t:<terrain kind>` to the Blender step to re-render a
subset (`w:` is an infantry unit's walk cycle, 48 frames; terrain tops are rendered only when
named; `f0`..`f7` limit a test render to those facings). The walk cycles of the seventeen
units on foot take about as long again as all the unit sheets together.

Buildings and units are modelled from real-world references, in metres: `scripts/hd_kit.py` is
the modelling kit and material palette, `hd_arch.py` the architectural parts, and
`hd_buildings*.py`, `hd_vehicles.py`, `hd_infantry.py`, `hd_aircraft.py` and `hd_naval.py` hold
one function per building or unit. `scripts/generate_hd_art.py` keeps the sprite canvas, the
quick flat Pillow preview (`python scripts/generate_hd_art.py [OUT_DIR]`) and the helpers of the
terrain tops.

The legacy generators remain for their validation fixtures. HD artwork is loaded by definition
ID, checked through the bounded PNG loader, and rendered at an explicit world size so texture
resolution never changes unit placement or building footprints. No simulation rules change.

Native renderer captures are in `docs/screenshots/hd/`, including the developed base and the
mixed-technology review setup. Run the visual-review command below to regenerate them.

## Maps

* **demo** (64 × 64): a rock ridge with a gap and a lake.
* **crossroads** (56 × 56): bases in opposite corners, three lanes between rock ridges and lakes.
* **riverlands** (64 × 48): bases on both banks of a river with three fords.

Large maps, painted at or near the largest size the engine draws (256 × 256 cells, four times the
area of the earlier 128 × 128 files), so that bases lie far apart: the first two start positions
are the two farthest from each other, 190 to 276 cells apart on most maps (up to 137 before). Each mixes a
settlement, open water and nature, and scatters landmarks over the country between the bases:
copses, ponds in their marsh, crags, hamlets, farms in their fields, oases, ruins, small ore
fields. Six to ten start positions in the file.

* **liberty_bay** (256 × 256): a metropolis of towers and city blocks split by a river between two
  bays; avenues and country bridges cross it; a port town with piers on each bay, marshes on the
  river banks, farmland in crop, woods, lakes and suburbs around.
* **frostline** (224 × 224): arctic snowfields and pine forests, a snow-capped mountain chain with
  three passes, thawed valleys with farms, frozen lakes, pack ice along two coasts of an arctic sea
  and an industrial town at the centre.
* **oasis_gulf** (256 × 192): desert dunes and sandstone mesas between two gulf coasts, palm
  oases, adobe villages, a farmed green belt along the highway and a harbour city with towers.
* **coral_archipelago** (256 × 256): tropical islands with beaches, jungle, palms and mangrove
  shallows, joined by broad causeways; a resort city on the central island. Naval power matters.
* **continental_divide** (256 × 256): every environment at once: snowy highlands with frozen
  lakes, desert with sea inlets and temperate farmland around a lake with an island city reached
  by four bridges.
* **steel_harbor** (256 × 256): a whole-map city of boulevards. A river winds between two harbour
  basins with piers and splits around a downtown island of towers; avenue bridges cross it and the
  bases sit in cleared industrial lots, with parks and ponds holding the contested ore.
* **glacier_pass** (240 × 240): green valleys with farms, woods and fjords on the west and east,
  divided by a snowy massif. A winding pass in the north and south, a narrow goat trail on each
  side and a central pass around a glacial lake rimmed with ice are the only ways across.
* **red_canyon** (192 × 192): a red sandstone plateau cut by canyons. Each base has its own basin;
  canyons lead to a ruined desert town around an oasis pool at the centre, and long rim canyons
  past palm oases join neighbouring bases as flanking routes.
* **jungle_delta** (256 × 224): a tropical river delta. A great lagoon with jungle islets fills the
  centre; distributaries lined with marsh and mangrove cut the jungle into islands joined by sand
  bars and timber bridges, with fishing villages and two river ports. Naval power controls the
  middle.

Ocean maps (six starts each). Their land is one piece without loops, so the sea is one piece too:
a fleet can sail from any shore to any other, while armies have a single way across.

* **pacific_atoll** (256 × 256): a ring of reef islands around a lagoon in the open ocean. Two
  channels cut the ring into two arcs with three bases each; a broad winding sand bar joins each
  arc to the island in the middle of the lagoon. Palm islets, jungle and fishing villages.
* **iron_isthmus** (256 × 224): two continents in the sea, joined by one neck of land with a port
  town on it. Capes, lakes in their marsh, farmland and woods, and a sand spit out to an ore
  island on each side. Fleets can sail around either continent and shell any coast.
* **arctic_floes** (240 × 240): islands of snow and rock in a polar sea, joined by shelves of sea
  ice that armies cross and nobody can build on, past icebergs. A broad shelf joins each home
  island to the middle island, the only way across by land; each home island also has a northern
  and a western side island of its own at the end of a narrower shelf.

Six maps made for the largest size, each around a kind of country the others do not have:

* **ember_caldera** (256 × 256): a volcanic island. A lava lake fills the crater; a ring wall of
  basalt with four passes encloses the caldera floor and its rich ore; four lava rivers run to the
  sea and cut the island into quarters, crossed only where the flow has cooled. Ash above, jungle
  and fishing villages below, the sea all around.
* **frozen_sound** (256 × 256): two snowbound shores and a sheet of sea ice between them, cut by
  leads of dark water and studded with icebergs; islands frozen into it hold ore and a ruined
  research station. The whole middle of the map is open manoeuvre ground where nothing can be
  built.
* **harvest_plains** (256 × 256): farmland to the horizon: a patchwork of grain fields, ploughed
  earth and pasture with hedgerows, tracks and villages. Open tank country; a river with marshy
  banks crosses the middle, with three bridges and two broad fords.
* **black_water** (256 × 256): a swamp. Nearly all of it is marsh, cut by bayous and mangrove
  thickets and crossed by straight timber causeways; the only building ground is on dry hummocks,
  one under each base and smaller ones worth fighting for. A drowned city lies in the middle.
* **thousand_lakes** (256 × 256): a lake district: lakes of every size, frozen on the snowy fells
  in two corners, and between them a web of necks and peninsulas with many ways round and none of
  them straight; roads cross the lakes on long bridges.
* **wheel_of_worlds** (256 × 256): ten sectors around a ruined city, two each of farmland, snow,
  volcanic ash, desert and jungle, with a ridge on every border that opens only at the hub and on
  the ring road. One base in every sector.

Five grounds came with these maps. **Ice** and **marsh** carry units but no buildings, so a
frozen lake or a swamp is ground to fight over, not to settle. **Lava** blocks armies and ships
alike. **Fields in crop** and **volcanic ash** are ordinary building ground with a look of their
own.

With MAP GROWTH on (see below) a map file of the largest size is played as it is by any number of
players (the smaller ones grow with the players, up to that size); start positions a file lacks
are made as before.

Cities, forests, mountains and houses block movement. Forests, houses, city blocks and towers
are **destructible scenery**: hover one to see its name and health, select armed units and
right-click it to destroy it (the cursor shows ATTACK). Trees fall quickly, towers take a while;
bombs and strategic weapons flatten what they cover. Nobody fires at scenery unasked. The cleared
cell becomes open, buildable ground, so you can cut a road through a forest or level a city block
for a base. Mountains and cliffs stay. Bridges carry land units over water but
ships cannot pass under them. On these maps computer armies grow with the distance they cover and
flank instead of meeting head-on.

### Map growth (an option, off at first)

As in Red Alert 2, a map is played as its file defines it, by you and as many computer players as
the file has further start positions: 3 to 9 enemies, depending on the map. The sizes above are
those of the files.

The menu's **Map growth** row changes that. With it **ON** every map can be played by 2 to 10
players, you and 1 to 9 computer players, and **every player beyond the second adds an eighth to
the map's width and height**, so ten players fight on a map twice as wide and high (four times
the ground): Crossroads grows from 56 × 56 to 112 × 112 cells; a map of 256 × 256 cells, the
largest a map can be, stays as it is. The map row of the menu shows the size for the players
chosen, e.g. `riverlands  88 x 66` with four enemies. The enlarged map is the same map,
stretched: every forest, river, city and ore field is where it was, larger, and all that was
connected stays connected, over land and by sea. The players beyond the start positions of the
file get bases of their own, made when the match starts: on dry ground reachable from yours, as
far from every other base as possible, in mirrored pairs so that they are as fair as the map,
each with cleared building ground and an ore field. The map depends only on the map chosen and
the number of players, so a restart plays on the same ground.

## Requirements

* macOS (version 1.0 targets macOS only).
* Rust through [rustup](https://rustup.rs); `rust-toolchain.toml` pins the toolchain (1.98.1),
  which rustup installs on first use.

## Building and running

```sh
cargo run --release -p game
```

The game opens on the main menu, which is the lobby of a skirmish. Every choice is a row with a
`<` and a `>` button.

**MATCH**

| Row | Choices (the first is what the menu opens with) |
|-----|--------------------------------------------------|
| Map | the 21 maps, with the size the match is played at |
| Enemy players | 1 up to the start positions of the map file less one; with Map growth on, 1 to 9 on every map |
| Team mode | **ALLIED AI**: the computer players fight you and never each other. **FREE FOR ALL**: everyone against everyone |
| Map growth | **OFF**, ON (see "Map growth" above) |
| Game speed | **1×**; 0.5× to 4×. At 1× the game runs as Red Alert 2 does at its nominal 15 frames a second; **2× corresponds to Red Alert 2 played at 30 frames per second** |
| Team color | your color; every other player gets one of its own |
| Credits | **10000**; 5000 to 10000 in steps of 100, for every player |
| Unit count | **10**; 0 to 10. It sets the starting units: with 10 an Allied player has 5 Grizzly tanks and 4 GIs, a Soviet player 4 Rhino tanks and 7 Conscripts; with 0 only the MCV |

**RULES**

| Row | Choices |
|-----|---------|
| MCV start | **ON**: every player starts with a Mobile Construction Vehicle and deploys it. OFF: with the Construction Yard already standing |
| Short game | **ON**: a player is defeated when it has no building and no MCV left. OFF (the long game): when it has no building and no unit left |
| Superweapons | **ON**; OFF: the Nuclear Missile Silo, Iron Curtain, Chronosphere and Weather Control Device cannot be built and are not listed |
| Crates | **ON**: a crate lies on the map and is replaced a few minutes after a unit drove onto it (money, a free vehicle, a rank, armour, firepower, speed, repairs, or the whole map explored) |

The **PLAYERS** panel has a line for every player of the match with its color. Yours has your
**country**: one of the nine, shown with its side, or **RANDOM** (America at first). Each computer
player has its own country (**RANDOM** at first) and its own **difficulty**: Easy, **Medium** or

Super Me, the unit speed, ALL UNLOCKED, Map growth and the game speed are this game's own; the

sound on or off, edge scrolling, and scroll speed; they are stored in
`~/Library/Application Support/MyGame001/settings.txt`.

## Vision and announcements

**FOG OF WAR defaults OFF.** Once you explore a cell, normal enemies and activity there remain
visible and targetable. With the option ON, enemies disappear when they leave current sight.
Spies, submerged units and stationary Mirage disguises still require a suitable detector.
A powered hostile Gap Generator restores circular shroud within 10 cells every tick, even
against a Spy Satellite; your own local sight counters it. A powered Psychic Sensor detects
disguises and displays enemy orders aimed within 15 cells as purple lines. Concealed sources
remain concealed. A powered Spy Satellite reveals the map outside gaps and prevents radar-spy
shroud resets. Ore growth is also configurable in the lobby (default ON).

Original synthesized motifs and text announce unit ready, construction complete, production
on hold, cannot deploy, base under attack, unit lost, low power, insufficient funds, superweapon
construction/readiness/launch and radar online/offline. Repeated categories are limited to one
per eight seconds, with one second between announcements. These are cues, without recorded speech.

### How to start and how to win

With the lobby's defaults you start with 10000 credits, an MCV and your starting units. Select
the MCV and press `D`: it becomes your Construction Yard where it stands (it needs clear,
buildable ground for a yard of 3 × 3 cells, which every start position has), and the Buildings
tab fills. Build a power plant and a refinery, then a Barracks and a War Factory.

**Short game** (the default): you defeat a player by destroying all of its buildings and its
MCVs; its other units do not save it. You are defeated the same way, so an MCV kept safe keeps
you in the match. **Long game** (Short game off): a player is defeated only when it has neither
a building nor any unit left. The end screen says which rule decided.

With several enemies every computer player has its own base, economy, AI and color. With **ALLIED
AI** they are allied against you: they never fire at each other or march on each other's bases,
and the match ends when only they are left. With **FREE FOR ALL** they fight each other too.

Buildings need power: under low power production slows down and the powered defences stop
firing (see "Production, power and base building" above). Harvesters bring ore to a refinery
for credits: a War Miner carries 40 bails and a Chrono Miner 20, a bail of ore pays 25 credits
and a bail of gems (the blue-green seams at the heart of the large fields of the open country)
50, and each Ore Purifier adds a quarter. A cell holds at most 12 bails. Ore slowly grows
denser and spreads onto the free ground beside a dense cell, and back onto ground a miner
emptied; gems do neither. Nothing can be built on ore. Selling a refinery returns 300 credits,
not half its price, so its free miner is not a bargain.
Buildings require visible, unoccupied terrain within two free cells of a base building of
yours (four for the cheap defences, eight for walls). Shipyards require a visible water
footprint touching shore within 31 free cells of your base (scout the water first; 18 of the 21
shipped maps have suitable water within that reach of the first start, and on the others you
build towards it); naval units stay in water, amphibious transports can use either, and
aircraft can cross land and water. Harriers and Black Eagles return to their dock at an Airforce
Command to reload.
The minimap shows the map only once you own a radar building.

Units move as fast as in Red Alert 2 at its nominal 15 frames a second, which is slower than this
game was before: raise the game speed (`]`) if you prefer the pace of the original at 30 frames
(2×) or more.

A new unit comes out beside its building. When every cell around the building is taken, it comes
out further away instead (the nearest free ground it could walk to, on the side of the rally
point), so a crowd around a factory never stops production; set a rally point to keep the exit
clear.

Aircraft, airships and rocketeers **fly**: they take off where they are built, cruise high above
the ground over their own shadow, and pass over units, buildings, cliffs and water. A selected
aircraft shows a plumb line down to the ground cell it is over. Harriers and Black Eagles land
on their dock at an Airforce Command while they reload.

A veteran shows one chevron and an elite unit three; when one of your units is promoted or a
crew gets out of a wreck, a notice in the top left corner says so.

When a player is defeated, a notice in the top left corner says so in that player's team colour
and with its country ("Red Player (Iraq) has been defeated"). Hovering an enemy unit or building
names its owner the same way.

```sh

```

`sh scripts/validate_ra2_parity.sh <label>` runs all eleven in this order and keeps their logs
under `docs/validation/ra2-parity/<label>/`.

`--smoke-test <frames>` runs the real game for that many frames, plays the menu flow, and exits
with code 0 only if every check passes: it measures the menu's layout with its longest texts,
starts four matches with the functions the menu's buttons call (the systems that read the
mouse are not driven; one match with most rows of the lobby changed, in which it deploys the
MCV, packs the yard up and deploys it again; its restart; one that starts with the Construction
Yards; one with the lobby's defaults), and compares each with its choices of countries, options
and rules (not with the chosen game speed and difficulty). The
benchmark times ticks of 200 fighting units against the budget in `docs/DECISIONS.md`; the soak
plays computer players against each other for 36,000 ticks on every map and pairing of the sides.
`m14_duels` plays computer players one against one to the end on every map (all pairings of sides
and difficulties with `full`, with the countries in turn; a few maps by naming them; `trace` to
see where a slow match stands) and reports the tick each match ended in: the first check after
adding or reshaping a map. The tests that play whole matches on every map use all cores.

### Skirmish options and battlefield controls

Super Me defaults off. When on,
only the selected human player pays **1 credit per production item** and advances construction
and unit production **10 times faster**. Power shortages, prerequisites, queue limits and
exit/placement rules still apply. With Super Me on, the menu's **Unit speed** row also makes
your units move up to 4 times faster; it is separate from the game speed, which speeds up
everyone. Cancel returns only credits actually spent. Restart retains
the choices; a new normal game uses the original balance data. AI players cannot receive the bonus.

Team paint uses blue, red, gold, green, purple, cyan, orange, pink, lime or ivory; computer player n receives the
n-th color after yours, so every player of a match has its own color. Unit/building faction accents, production icons and radar markers use
these colors, including after capture or mind control. Metal, glass and ground remain natural.

Edge scrolling is optional and defaults off for new settings. Existing settings are preserved.
The held camera gesture works away from window edges; wheel zoom ignores the sidebar. Arrow
keys and minimap navigation remain available. `A` over ore starts the selected miners' cycles;
other selected units still attack-move. A direct Move, Stop or enemy Attack overrides mining.

The maps add connected roads, blocked woodland and civilian blocks, cliff terraces, shoreline
animation and broad ground-color variation. Bases retain clear building aprons and existing ore
fields. The shared layout writes obstacles to TMX so visible obstacles and pathfinding agree.
Cliffs are blocked terraces; this does not add traversable height levels or garrisonable civilians.
