### Changed in M8: original visual polish

All eight building sprites and all eleven unit sheets were redrawn by the repository generators.
`examples/art/painter.rs` supplies deterministic polygon, cylinder and isometric box primitives;
`examples/art/terrain.rs` supplies grass, mineral, water and stone textures used by all three
shipped maps. No third-party game image, sound, design file or logo was imported. Runtime team
paint recolouring (`src/presentation.rs`) preserves metal, concrete, armour, glass and shadows.
The command interface uses the same checked sprites for its icons. `docs/screenshots/m8-*.png`
are native renderer captures of this original game, generated with `MYGAME_VISUAL_REVIEW`.
The prior generated art and audio were rejected as final by the user; this visual revision still
requires user verification. Synthesised audio is unchanged and remains nonfinal.

### M9 production extension

`data/arsenal.ron` and the additions to `data/units.ron`, `data/economy.ron`, and
`data/combat.ron` were original data authored in `scripts/generate_arsenal.py` for this
repository. Since RA1 of the RA2 parity programme every file in `data/` is written by
`scripts/generate_ra2_data.py` from the committed reference tables in
`crates/sim/tests/reference/` (numbers, flags and identifiers taken from the vanilla Red Alert
2 `rules.ini`; provenance in `docs/DECISIONS.md` R2 and R9) and from that script's own tables of
game specific values (labels, descriptions, roles, build times, weapons, the computer
player's plans). `data/rules.ron` is new in RA1. No ini file and no text of one is
distributed. Requested RA2 unit names identify gameplay roles; no third-party sprites,
audio, logos, or game data files are distributed. Existing repository-generated sprites
are reused as role illustrations. Map schema 2 adds explicit water terrain through the
existing repository map generators.

### HD presentation rebuild — 2026-10-05

The following original RGBA assets are generated from procedural geometry authored in
`scripts/generate_hd_art.py` and `scripts/generate_hd_terrain.py`. Since the RA2-style
pre-render pass (2026-10-05), buildings, units and terrain tops (ore, cliffs, roads, trees,
cottages) are rendered by `scripts/render_hd_blender.py` in Blender Cycles (bevelled geometry,
physically based materials, procedural grime, ambient occlusion and a sun shadow on a shadow
catcher) and composed by `scripts/finish_hd_art.py`, which also encodes team paint from a mask
pass. Grass, water and soil layers are world-periodic procedural textures from
`scripts/generate_hd_terrain.py` (Python, Pillow, NumPy), which also makes the particle
textures. Units have eight facings at four texture pixels per world pixel. No extracted game
assets or downloaded artwork are included; the earlier Pillow-rasterized set is superseded.

Faction architecture and environments pass (2026-10-05): buildings were remodelled in
`scripts/generate_hd_art.py` with original RA2-inspired silhouettes and faction materials, ships
and aircraft received new hulls and airframes, and the terrain gained sand, snow and pavement
layers (`terrain/8.png` to `terrain/10.png`) and rendered tops for snowy mountains, mesas, snowy
pines, palms, bridges, city blocks, towers, snowy cottages and adobe houses (`terrain/11.png` to
`terrain/19.png`). All of it is generated by the same scripts; nothing is copied from a game.

Flight pass (2026-10-09): the five units of the air domain (`harrier`, `black_eagle`,
`nighthawk`, `kirov`, `rocketeer`) were re-rendered by `scripts/render_hd_blender.py` without the
baked ground shadow. The game draws them high above the ground and casts their shadow itself, as
a darkened copy of the same sprite on the ground below (`crates/game/src/units.rs`).

Spawned missiles (2026-10-12, RA2 parity programme RA4): the V3 rocket and the Dreadnought's
missile are units that fly and can be shot down, so each has a sheet of eight facings of its own
(`sprites/hd/units/v3_rocket.png`, `sprites/hd/units/dred_missile.png`), modelled in
`scripts/hd_aircraft.py` (`v3_rocket`, `dred_missile`: original geometry, proportioned after
an artillery rocket and a ship-launched cruise missile, with the owner's band round the body)
and rendered by `scripts/render_hd_blender.py` and `scripts/finish_hd_art.py` like every other
aircraft. The Hornet and the Osprey show the sheets of the Harrier and the NightHawk
(`assets/data/arsenal.ron`, `art`).

Realistic redesign (2026-10-10): every building and unit was remodelled from real-world
reference dimensions, in metres, with original geometry written for this project:
`scripts/hd_kit.py` (modelling kit and material palette), `scripts/hd_arch.py` (architectural
parts), `scripts/hd_buildings.py`, `hd_buildings_a.py`, `hd_buildings_b.py`, `hd_buildings_c.py`,
`scripts/hd_vehicles.py`, `hd_infantry.py`, `hd_aircraft.py` and `hd_naval.py`.
`scripts/render_hd_blender.py` renders them with one physically based shader per material class
(painted steel, concrete, brick, corrugated sheet, roofing, tarmac, glass, rubber, cloth, lamps).
Real vehicles, ships, aircraft and installations served only as reference for proportions; no
model, texture, photograph or game asset was copied or downloaded. The terrain tops are unchanged.

Largest maps (2026-10-11): five more ground layers, sea ice, grain fields, marsh, volcanic ash
and lava (`terrain/20.png` to `terrain/24.png`), are world-periodic procedural textures from
`scripts/generate_hd_terrain.py` (`ice`, `crops`, `marsh`, `ash`, `lava`), made like the soil, sand
and snow layers; nothing is copied from a game or downloaded.
